Paid media · Lead quality

We’re hitting our CPA target. Sales says lead quality is falling.

A reader asked Search Engine Journal’s Ask a PPC column almost exactly this:

“hitting our CPA target, but the sales team says the quality is getting worse”

A PPC reader, Search Engine Journal

Here’s the uncomfortable part: both sides can be right. The ads account counts what it was told to count, and it may be counting correctly. Sales counts people they can actually help. Those two numbers can drift apart without anyone doing their job badly.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

Why it happens

01

The account may count every lead the same

“Every form submission was being counted as the same primary conversion”

A PPC practitioner who compared conversions with the CRM, r/PPC

When a real request, a quick question and a spam form all count as one lead, bidding can learn to find more of whatever is cheapest to count. The cost per lead goes down. The buyers may not get better.

02

The target is the cost of a lead, not the cost of a customer

“For us, a $300 CPL puts the channel CAC at $600+.”

A plumbing company's marketer, r/PPC

That’s one company’s math, not a benchmark. The point is that a lead target and a customer cost are two different numbers, and only one of them shows up in the ads account.

03

Something after the call changed

Capacity, response time, who answers the phone, a new visit fee. If the step after the lead got slower or harder, quality can look worse even when the same people are coming in.

Four steps before you change bids or vendors

  1. Agree on four words with sales: inquiry, qualified inquiry, appointment and sale. Write them down.
  2. Pick one group of leads: one campaign, one recent month. Ask sales what happened to each one, and mark the leads nobody can match instead of dropping them.
  3. Look at what the account counts: whether a phone call, a form and a spam form all count the same, and which one bidding is learning from.
  4. Then choose a route: fix it with the current team, get an independent diagnosis, or hire ongoing help.

Hold bidding changes until step three is settled. Bidding learns from whatever you count, so changing it first can teach the account the wrong lesson faster.
