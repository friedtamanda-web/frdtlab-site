Issue zero

Google now learns from the conversions you told it to ignore

Issue zero of The Operator Brief. Smart Bidding and secondary conversions, the Google Ads Editor 2.14 defaults, and the Local Services Ads move to Performance Max. Changes reported November 11 to 23, 2026.

Somewhere in your Google Ads account there’s an action like “Clicked call button,” set to secondary because half those clicks never became a call. Secondary meant “count it, don’t chase it.” Google’s help page now says Smart Bidding learns from those actions too, with no switch to turn it off. That’s the big one this issue. The other two are smaller, but one of them has a clock on it.

1. Smart Bidding now learns from your secondary conversions

What changed: Google calls it Search Journey Awareness. Search campaigns on Target CPA, Target ROAS, Maximize Conversions or Maximize Conversion Value now learn from conversion actions you aren’t bidding to. Google’s page says there’s nothing to opt into and no on/off switch. A weekly roundup reported it November 17; Google’s page carries no date.

What I’m not sure of: Google doesn’t say how much weight a secondary action carries.

Check this week: open Goals, filter to secondary actions, and write next to each one whether it means a real lead. Any that don’t are now teaching your bidding. Since the weight is unknown, this is a review, not a panic. Google’s page

2. New Search campaigns built in Editor start with AI Max on

Google Ads Editor 2.14 turns on AI Max, text asset automation and Final URL expansion for every new Search campaign you build in it (reported November 12). Final URL expansion can send a click to a page you didn’t pick, like a blog post with no form. Check all three settings before you post a new campaign from Editor. Editor 2.14 release notes

3. Local Services Ads are moving into Performance Max, on a clock

The clock: Google emails you 14 days before your account moves, and broader groups are moving in late 2026, which is now. Select US home and storefront services went first in August.

What changed: LSA campaigns move on their own into a Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals, still showing on Search and Maps. Manual bidding goes away. Your old LSA reports don’t carry over, and the download Google offers holds lead totals split by phone, message and booking. No impressions, no clicks.

My recommendation, not Google’s: if you run LSA, pull what you can from your current reports now, before the 14-day email lands. Google’s transition page

On watch

Meta Leads campaigns losing placement choice. Reported, not confirmed by Meta yet. I’ll tell you when it is.

“Learning” ends at 7 days. Learning doesn’t. Google’s page says the Learning label never lasts more than 7 days, and also that bidding takes one to two conversion cycles to settle on a new goal. If your leads take three weeks to close, don’t judge a change on day 8.

The first skill

The Junk Lead Sorter

You export a week of form fills and call notes. The skill reads each one and tags it likely job, junk or spam, with a one-line reason you can check.

What you get back:

  • Your lead list with the tag and reason added to each row
  • The junk rate by campaign and by source, so you see which spend brings the noise
  • A clean list of the real leads to send back to Google Ads as offline conversions, if your form or call tracking captures the Google click ID (GCLID). Without it, you still get the tags and the junk rate.

What it reads: the names, phone numbers, emails and messages in the file you give it, inside your own Claude account. Leave out any column you don’t want it to see.

Why it’s first: item 1 above. If bidding now learns from more of your signals, the signals had better be real leads.

Runs in: Claude, as a skill, on any Claude plan including Free, with code execution turned on. Ships with install notes, sample data and its known limits, like misreading a short message from a real customer as junk.
