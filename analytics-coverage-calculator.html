Where the gap comes from

Low coverage is almost always wiring, not the internet.

  • The purchase tag doesn't fire on every checkout path. Express checkout, a payment redirect, or a thank-you page that some orders never reach.
  • Orders that never touch the website. Phone orders, invoices, marketplace sales, and repeat orders placed by your team.
  • Consent and blockers. The honest, expected slice. Usually single digits, not half your revenue.
  • Mismatched definitions. Analytics counts revenue before tax and shipping; your books count it after refunds. Same store, two different numbers.
  • Duplicate conversion actions. Two purchase events both counted as primary, so the ad platform reports more than happened while analytics reports less.

Here's why it matters beyond reporting: your ad platforms bid on what tracking reports, not on your books. If tracking sees 40% of sales, automated bidding learns from 40% of your customers and grades itself on a return that isn't real in either direction.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What is analytics revenue coverage?

The share of your real revenue that your analytics tool can see. Divide the revenue your analytics reports by the revenue in your books or store platform for the same dates. 100% means it sees everything. 42% means more than half your sales are invisible to it.

What's a normal coverage number?

Somewhere around 85% to 95% is typical for a well-wired store. Consent banners, ad blockers, and people switching devices always cost a little. Under 85% usually means something in the setup, not just the internet being the internet.

Why does low coverage matter if my store platform has the right number?

Because your ad platforms bid on what analytics and tracking report, not on your books. If tracking sees 40% of sales, automated bidding is optimizing toward 40% of reality and reporting a return that isn't real in either direction.

What if my coverage is over 100%?

Your analytics is counting sales that didn't happen. The usual causes are duplicate purchase events, test orders, refunds and cancellations never deducted, or tax and shipping counted on one side and not the other.
