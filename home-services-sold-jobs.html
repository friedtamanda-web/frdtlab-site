Home services: which leads turned into sold jobs?

The ad report shows leads. Your board shows sold jobs. In between are the calls nobody picked up, the addresses you don’t serve, and the appointments that never happened. Here’s how to see where yours drop off.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

“Anyone dealing with big numbers of spam leads on Google ads lately”

A poster asking about home services, r/PPC

  • Count the leads you can’t match in their own row instead of dropping them.
  • Stop at the last step you can confirm. A shaky last column is worse than an honest shorter table.
  • Look at one ad channel at a time.

Three things that trip up home services

01

An appointment isn’t a sold job

Keep the two columns apart. Say “booked appointments” only when you mean appointments, and “sold” only when the job is signed or paid.

“he charges for site visits, which seems to push away many prospects”

A practitioner guessing at a decking client’s drop-off, r/googleads
02

Area and job type come before intake

Check “do we serve this address?” and “do we do this job?” before calling a lead an intake problem. If you run Local Services Ads, keep those leads separate from Search in the first comparison.

03

Compare like with like

For heating and cooling, compare emergency calls with emergency calls and planned replacements with planned replacements. Note unusual weather, after-hours coverage and how full the schedule was. Season matters, but it doesn’t explain any single campaign on its own.
