Building products: which quote requests turned into orders?

Which campaigns brought in quote requests your team could actually work, and which of those turned into orders? When the ad account stops at the form, that second question goes unanswered. Here’s a way to answer it.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

“If someone inquires using gmail or hotmail they treat them as junk leads.”

An in-house marketer at a B2B seller describing a management rule, r/PPC

Line up one group of leads

One campaign, one recent month, the same leads all the way across:

  • Count the leads you can’t match in their own row instead of dropping them.
  • Stop at the last step you can confirm. A shaky last column is worse than an honest shorter table.
  • Look at one ad channel at a time.

That rule may be right for some sellers and wrong for others. A contractor using a personal email can still place a real order. Check what got set aside before deciding the ads sent junk.

Where quote-driven sales get blurry

01

Every form may count the same

“Every form submission was being counted as the same primary conversion”

A PPC practitioner who compared conversions with the CRM, r/PPC

A spec question, a job-seeker and a real quote request can all land in one bucket. Ask which forms the account counts, and which one bidding is learning from.

02

Orders take longer than a month

Quotes can sit for weeks. Mark still-open quotes as pending instead of counting them as lost, and look again at a set date.

03

Some orders arrive another way

If a quote turns into an order through a dealer, a rep or a phone call, it may never be tied back to the ad. Show those as unmatched rather than leaving them out.
