You paid for leads. How many were real jobs you could actually take?

“our impressions are improving every time we talk but the phone isnt ringing.”

A heating and cooling company owner, Reddit

If the report keeps getting better and the phone doesn’t, you’re not imagining it. The report counts what it was set up to count. You count jobs on the calendar. Here’s how to find out which of the two is telling you more, using records you probably already have.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

Three questions to ask about last month

Pick one month and one service. Pull your call log, your form emails or your booking list. Then answer three questions:

01

How many were real people who wanted to reach you?

“not a single call receive from customer”

An advertiser describing spend with no customer calls, Google Ads Community
If the answer is “not many”
Spam, wrong numbers or form fills nobody remembers sending usually point to what the account is counting, not to the market drying up.
02

How many were in your area, for a job you do?

“don't pick up, aren't even in our area”

A fencing advertiser, r/googleads
If the answer is “not many”
Check the location and service settings before blaming whoever answers the phone. Two questions per lead: do we serve this address, and do we do this job?
03

How many did you book, quote or sell?

If real, local leads came in but few turned into work
The ads may be doing their part. Look at what happened after the call: how fast someone called back, whether a visit got scheduled, what the quote looked like.

Write the three numbers down next to what you spent. That one line usually tells you more than a month of reports.

Before you fire anyone

The person running your ads may be counting exactly what they were asked to count. The question is whether it’s the thing you care about. A few calm checks first:

  • Whose account is it? You should own the ad account and its history, even if someone else runs it.

    “do i give them my google ads account or would they use their own?”

    An advertiser weighing a hire, r/googleads
  • What counts as a lead? Ask for the list of actions the account counts. A two-second call and a booked estimate shouldn’t count the same.
  • Can you hear the calls? If calls go through a tracking number, you should be able to see or hear them.

    “I had to buy my own call tracking software”

    A masonry and restoration advertiser, r/PPC

Sometimes the answer is just time

Not every owner needs a deep review. If the numbers check out and you simply can’t keep up with it, that’s a fair reason to hand it off, as long as you keep the account in your name.

“do not have time to learn Google ads”

An operator comparing agencies and specialists, r/smallbusiness

If the three numbers don’t add up and nobody can tell you why, that’s when an independent look is worth it, before you change spend or switch vendors.
