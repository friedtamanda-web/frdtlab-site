Personal injury: which inquiries became signed cases?

The ad account counts calls and forms. Your intake team counts signed cases. When the two numbers move in different directions, you need to see the same inquiries in both places before deciding what to change.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

“provide more closed case volume and decrease the overall cost per case”

A senior marketing manager at a personal injury firm, Clutch review

  • Count the leads you can’t match in their own row instead of dropping them.
  • Stop at the last step you can confirm. A shaky last column is worse than an honest shorter table.
  • Look at one ad channel at a time.

That goal is about cases, not inquiries. A lower cost per inquiry only helps if those inquiries sign.

Where injury inquiries fall off

01

Not every caller has a case you take

Separate callers whose matter is outside your practice area or where you don’t practice from callers who were a fit and didn’t sign. They point to different fixes: the ad’s targeting in one case, the intake conversation in the other.

02

Keep channels apart at first

If you run Local Services Ads and Search, compare them separately before adding them together. They reach people at different moments.

03

Signed takes time

Mark inquiries still in review as pending, not lost, and look again at a set date.
