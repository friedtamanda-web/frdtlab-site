01THE SITUATION

A specialist retailer. An invisible presence.

Framewell — a specialty replacement-parts retailer with a ~1,800-SKU catalog of dimensional and fitment-specific home hardware — was losing search visibility to big-box competitors across every surface that mattered.

Their rank tracker showed organic position. Their content tools prescribed more words. Neither explained why retailers with a fraction of Framewell's product depth were consistently appearing in AI-generated answers for the exact queries Framewell should own.

The question wasn't whether they ranked. They ranked. The question was why ranking no longer meant being the answer — and what it would take to close that gap before the AI search transition made it permanent.

02THE PROBLEM

Three compounding problems. One visible symptom.

AI-generated answers were absorbing buying intent before any click happened. Ranking on page one no longer guaranteed visibility. Framewell needed to know exactly what was blocking them — not another prescription to write more words.

PROBLEM A

Entity invisibility

AI models had no signal to recognize Framewell as a subject-matter authority. To the models, it was a product listing — not an expert source. Citations never followed, no matter how well individual pages ranked.

PROBLEM B

Semantic coverage gaps

Critical product concepts — compatibility tables, dimensional fitment guides, installation context — were absent from the site's content architecture. AI engines cannot retrieve what isn't structured to be retrieved.

PROBLEM C

Competitive blind spots

No visibility into which competitor pages AI engines were retrieving — or what structural signals made their content trustworthy to models. Flying blind against an AI-first SERP while competitors quietly built retrieval authority.

The rank tracker said they were visible. The AI search layer said they didn't exist. Both were correct — and that's the gap SERPrecon was built to close.

04KEY FINDINGS

What the data actually showed.

FINDING 01

AI models read Framewell as a store, not a source

The fix wasn't more product copy. It was structured specificity: dimensional data formatted for extraction, compatibility tables AI could parse, and installation context that answered the fitment question — the one question big-box sites routinely skipped.

FINDING 03

Google AI Overviews rewarded fitment content, not purchase content

For "replacement [product type]" queries, AI Overviews pulled exclusively from content that answered "will this fit?" — not "add to cart." At the time of the audit, Framewell's product pages answered neither in a format models could extract.

FINDING 02

Three domains dominated AI citations for queries Framewell ranked for organically

All three shared one structural signal: explicit replacement-intent framing throughout their taxonomy. Not just product pages — category architecture, navigation, and internal linking all signaled "this site answers the replacement question."

FINDING 04

Fan-out revealed 83 content opportunities no competitor had fully claimed

Fan-out mapping exposed an entire semantic neighborhood — measurement guides, brand-specific compatibility hubs, installation subtype expansions — that no competitor had fully owned. First-mover advantage was still available. For now.
