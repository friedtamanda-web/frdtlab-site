The problem

The most expensive mistake in search is fixing the wrong problem.

Most teams respond to invisibility the same way: rewrite the page, add words, chase a score. But pages fail for three different reasons — and two of them can't be fixed with copy. If you don't know which problem you have, every hour of optimization is a guess.

Your rank tracker

Tells you that you dropped. Not why. You're watching the scoreboard with no game film.

Your content tool

Grades your words against everyone else's words — and always prescribes the same medicine: more words.

Your AI monitor

Tells you ChatGPT didn't mention you. Then leaves. No diagnosis, no fix, no next step.

Rationale — problem sectionNew section; nothing like it exists on the current site. It names the tension in the buyer's own workflow and positions the three adjacent tool categories (rank trackers, content scorers, AI monitors) as incomplete — which sets up SERPrecon as the connective layer rather than a fourth point tool.
The Visibility Triage

Every invisible page has one of three problems. SERPrecon tells you which.

We score your page the way engines score it — semantic and lexical relevance, intent classification, and authority signals — against every page that's winning your query.

1 · Wrong words

Content gap. You're missing the entities, questions, and phrasing the winners cover. This is the only problem copy can fix — and SERPrecon gives you the exact gap list, not a grade.

2 · Wrong framing

Intent mismatch. Your page sells while the SERP compares — or explains while the SERP sells. More words make this worse. SERPrecon shows the intent the result set actually rewards.

3 · Not enough trust

Authority gap. Your content already beats the average and you still don't rank. No rewrite fixes this. SERPrecon proves it — so you invest in links and architecture instead of edits.

Rationale — framework sectionThe data: in the proving engagement, every one of the 83 documented gaps resolved to one of a small set of failing layers, and diagnosis-before-build was what killed instinct spending. A three-way verdict (content, intent, authority) is that same discipline packaged for the buyer. This is the category-claiming move. The current site lists seven features with no organizing idea. A named framework (“The Visibility Triage”) turns the product's real behavior — separating content vs. intent vs. authority failures — into ownable language that survives screenshots, talks, and LLM citation. It also makes the anti-pitch explicit: sometimes the answer is “stop rewriting,” which no content-scoring competitor can say.
