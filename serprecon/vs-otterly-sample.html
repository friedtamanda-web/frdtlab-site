What Otterly genuinely does well

Credit where it's due: Otterly made AI answer monitoring accessible before most of the market knew to want it. The prompt-tracking workflow is simple, the pricing is friendly, and if your question is "does ChatGPT mention us this week," it answers that question without ceremony. Plenty of teams should start there.

Where teams outgrow it: the morning the dashboard shows a competitor being recommended instead of you — and there's no next screen. Monitoring tells you the score. It doesn't tell you whether you're losing on content, on intent, or on authority, and those three problems have three completely different fixes with three completely different costs.

Where SERPrecon goes further

SERPrecon reads the whole results layer — classic Google SERPs and AI answers — and separates every invisible page into one of three problems: wrong words (content gap), wrong framing (intent mismatch), or not enough trust (authority gap). That's the difference between knowing you lost and knowing what it costs to win.

One real pattern from a 16-month engagement: a page that beat the SERP's average content score and still didn't rank. Every content tool said "rewrite." The diagnosis said the words were fine — authority was the blocker. The team stopped rewriting, and the effort moved to where the data pointed. That verdict — stop rewriting — is something a monitoring tool can't produce, because it requires reading the page against the full result set, not counting mentions.

Common questions

Can I use Otterly and SERPrecon together?

Yes, and some teams do: Otterly for cheap wide monitoring, SERPrecon when a page or query needs diagnosis. If budget forces a choice, decide whether your bottleneck is knowing the score or knowing the fix.

Does SERPrecon track ChatGPT and Perplexity like Otterly does?

Yes — share-of-voice tracking across Google, ChatGPT, and Perplexity is part of the Monitor plan ($149/mo). The $49 plan covers page diagnosis with Google share-of-voice runs.

Is SERPrecon harder to use?

It reports more, so there's more to read. Every diagnosis leads with the verdict — which of the three problems this page has — before the supporting detail, so the first screen is a decision, not a spreadsheet.
