Why it exists

Most agent problems aren't model problems.

The agent was capable. The rules around it lived in someone's head, and that someone was busy. Here's what tends to go wrong without a written layer.

  • Money moves with no name on it.A budget goes up or a campaign gets paused overnight, and on Monday nobody can say who approved it or why.
  • Numbers arrive without a source.A report looks clean until a client asks where one figure came from, and the honest answer is "the agent wrote it."
  • Tasks stall quietly, or run twice.One job sits at "running" for a day. Two others do the same work, and the second undoes the first.
  • Work goes live that no human read.A post, a redirect, a reply to a customer. Fine ninety times. The other times are public.
  • The client notices before you do.A feed breaks or tracking drops, and the first alert is an email that starts with "quick question."

What exactly do I get?

A guided worksheet of 29 questions in five blocks, five fill-in templates your answers turn into (agent register and permission matrix, approval gates, report QA checklist, logging rules, incident flag), and ten stress-test scenarios with a 30-day review. It opens in your browser and prints cleanly if you prefer a pen.

Do I need to be technical?

No. It's written for people who run paid, SEO, and social, not for engineers. If you can describe what your automations touch and who should approve what, you can finish it.

How long does it take?

Plan on an afternoon for the worksheet. The templates fill themselves in from your answers, so most of the time goes into the decisions, not the formatting.

Which AI tools does it work with?

All of them. The kit governs actions, accounts, and approvals, not a specific product. Paste the finished matrix, gates, and flag format into whatever standing instructions your agents read before they act.
