Paid media · From lead to job

The ad report says leads are up. Which ones turned into jobs?

“our impressions are improving every time we talk but the phone isnt ringing.”

A heating and cooling company owner, Reddit

The ad report usually stops at the lead. Your team books, quotes and sells jobs after that, somewhere the report never looks. Here’s a simple way to line the two up so you can see what to fix first.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

Line them up side by side

Same campaign, same period, same group of leads all the way across:

What the ads reportReal calls and formsGood-fit inquiriesAppointments bookedJobs sold
From the ads accountPeople who actually reached youRight area, right jobOn the calendarSigned or paid
  • Show the leads you can’t match: count them in their own row instead of dropping them.
  • Keep appointments and sales apart: a booked appointment isn’t a sold job.
  • Stop at the last step you can confirm: if sales data is shaky, the appointment column is still useful.

Change the last column to fit your business: sold jobs for home services, orders for building products, signed cases for a law firm.

Where the numbers fall off points to the fix

Biggest dropLook here first
Ads report → real calls and formsWhat the account counts, and spam on the form
Real → good fitLocation, services and the offer in the ad
Good fit → appointmentResponse time, scheduling and capacity
Appointment → soldThe visit, the quote and pricing, which ads can’t fix

You may already have the answer

Some teams can already follow a lead all the way to a sale. Their problem isn’t seeing it, it’s a step they don’t run.

“I don't control the consultants' calendars.”

An in-house window and roofing marketer, r/PPC

If that sounds like you, you likely don’t need a tracking rebuild. When booked appointments don’t happen, find out why before you pay for more leads, and get the owner of that step into the conversation.
