Fix it in-house, get a diagnosis, or hire ongoing help: which one fits?

Leads are coming in and the sales aren’t following. Before anyone cuts spend or changes vendors, there are really only three routes. Which one fits depends on one thing: do you already know where leads stop?

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

The short answer

Fix it with your current team if you know where leads stall and someone can own the fix. If you can’t tell whether the problem is the ads, what gets counted, or what happens after the call, get a fixed-scope diagnosis before changing spend or vendors.

Three routes side by side

RouteChoose it whenWhat it costs you, and what you get
Fix it with your current teamThe break is visible and someone has time to act on itTeam time. Ship one fix, then check the affected step against where it was before.
Get an independent diagnosisThe records don’t agree, or nobody can isolate the causeA one-time fee. Findings, what’s still unknown, and fixes in order. Doing the fixes is separate.
Hire ongoing helpThe problem is clear, needs recurring work, and is more than your team can carryA recurring fee. Someone owns the agreed work and scheduled reviews. You keep ownership of the account and approval of the budget.

You may not need a rebuild

Some teams can already follow a lead from first call to sale. What’s missing is control over one step, not more data.

“I don't control the consultants' calendars.”

An in-house window and roofing marketer, r/PPC

If that’s you, the first route is probably right. The fix is getting the owner of that step into the conversation, not paying anyone to rebuild your tracking.

Doing it yourselves is a real option

“Would it make more sense for us to do this ourselves?”

A two-person startup weighing proposals, r/smallbusiness

Often, yes. A diagnosis earns its fee only when the records disagree and nobody can say why. If you can say why, spend the money on the fix.

What to bring to the decision

For one campaign or location, line up the same group of leads across:

  • Spend for the period
  • Leads the ads reported
  • Good-fit inquiries your team agrees were real, in your area, for a job you do
  • Confirmed sales or orders from that same group, with unmatched and still-pending leads shown in their own row

Note the market, the type of job, the one ad channel you’re looking at, and anything that limited capacity that month, like booked-out schedules or weather.

Leave with three things decided

A route, one person who owns it, and a date to look at the result. If you pick a diagnosis, set a fee cap and give read-only access. Don’t sign a retainer to find out what’s wrong.
