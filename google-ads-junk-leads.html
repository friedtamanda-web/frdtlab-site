Why are my Google Ads leads junk?

The form fills come in, the phone rings, and your team says most of it is junk. Before you cut spend or change vendors, it helps to know which kind of junk you have. “Bad lead” often covers four different problems, and each one has a different fix.

By Amanda Friedt · Updated November 27, 2026

Four kinds of junk, and how to tell them apart

01

Fake or mismatched form fills

“who then respond that they never filled out a form”

An agency practitioner, r/Google_Ads

“Anyone dealing with big numbers of spam leads on Google ads lately”

A poster asking about home services, r/PPC
What it may point to
Bots or form abuse, or an account that counts every submission as a lead. One practitioner found it by comparing the ads account with their CRM: “Every form submission was being counted as the same primary conversion”.
How to check
Pull 20 recent form fills and see how many came from real people who meant to reach you. Then look at which actions your ads account counts as a conversion.
02

Not in your area, or not a job you do

“don't pick up, aren't even in our area”

A fencing advertiser, r/googleads
What it may point to
Location or service settings, more likely than your intake team.
How to check
Ask two questions of each lead: do we serve this address, and do we do this job? If you also run Local Services Ads, keep those leads separate from Search while you check.
03

Real people your team set aside

“If someone inquires using gmail or hotmail they treat them as junk leads.”

An in-house marketer describing a management rule, r/PPC
What it may point to
A sorting rule, not the ads. Some of what gets labeled junk may be good-fit buyers who aren’t ready yet, or who don’t look the way the team expects.
How to check
Look at what got thrown out and why. Was it the lead, or the rule?
04

Good leads that stop later

“I don't control the consultants' calendars.”

An in-house window and roofing marketer, r/PPC

“he charges for site visits, which seems to push away many prospects”

A practitioner guessing at a decking client's drop-off, r/googleads
What it may point to
What happens after the call: response time, scheduling, a visit fee, a missed appointment.
How to check
Follow a handful of leads from first contact to appointment, quote and sale. Note where most of them stop, and who owns that step.

The 30-minute sort

Take one campaign and one recent month. Pull 20 to 30 inquiries and put each one in a pile:

  • Fake or never asked: start with the form and what the account counts.
  • Wrong area or wrong job: start with location and service settings.
  • Real, set aside by us: start with your sorting rules.
  • Real, stopped later: start with what happens after the call.
  • Real, sold: keep these. They tell you what a good lead looks like.

The biggest pile tells you where to look first. It may also tell you the ads are doing their job and the leak is somewhere else. That’s worth knowing before you pay anyone to rebuild anything.

A note on numbers that look great

When the dashboard says leads are way up and nobody on the team can feel it, believe the team long enough to check.

“conversions numbers are good but they are 100% too good to be true”

A commenter on display ad results, Hacker News
