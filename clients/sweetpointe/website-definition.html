The Platform Decision

You run Lightspeed Retail — it's your register, your inventory, your day. The real question isn't "which website builder," it's how to get a modern, findable site without breaking what already works. We looked at four paths.

The finding: Lightspeed's own eCom already covers the commerce SEO basics — clean URLs, an auto-generated sitemap, custom meta titles/descriptions, 301 redirects, and image alt text — and it keeps inventory synced to your POS as one system. Its one real gap is that it doesn't add structured-data / schema on its own — and schema is exactly what wins AI answers. So we build on Lightspeed and inject the GEO layer ourselves. If, once we're in, E-Series can't carry the custom pages and schema we need, we drop to the fallback — a fast marketing site for the get-found pages that links into your Lightspeed store for catalog and checkout. Either way, you stay on Lightspeed.

Why not Shopify: re-platforming means either abandoning the Lightspeed POS your team already runs, or running two inventory systems that fight each other (double entry, sync conflicts). That's cost and risk a seven-week-old shop doesn't need — and it's the opposite of "small site, all foundations."

The Foundations — Day-One Checklist

  • GA4 + Search Console verified and tracking the four conversions
  • Google Business Profile completed, categories Candy Store + Event Venue, weekly posts, monthly photos
  • Consistent NAP (name/address/phone) everywhere — small mismatches cut AI trust
  • Clean URLs + auto sitemap + robots (native to Lightspeed eCom)
  • LocalBusiness / CandyStore schema — the core identifier AI reads
  • Product + Offer schema on boxes; Event schema on parties/seasonal
  • FAQPage schema on the FAQ hub and key pages
  • Core Web Vitals — mobile-first, fast; ~80% of visits are phones
  • Review engine — request within 24h, respond within 48h

The schema stack is the piece Lightspeed doesn't do natively — it's injected as JSON-LD. It's low-cost, mostly one-time, and it's what turns "a nice site" into "the store the AI names."

Getting Named in AI Answers (GEO)

Over half of searches now trigger an AI summary that cites a handful of sources. If you're not structured to be retrieved, you're invisible in the research phase. The fix is cheap and mostly one-time.

  • Answer first. A direct 40–80 word answer under each page's headline — AI lifts concise blocks.
  • Question-shaped headings. "How much is a candy birthday party in Grosse Pointe?" mirrors how people actually ask.
  • The FAQ hub built from real customer questions — allergies, party pricing, delivery radius, corporate minimums, retro finds.
  • Facts AI can cite — prices, capacities, hours, the 1932 story, the service area — in plain, extractable text.

Baseline to beat. Today, asking ChatGPT / Google AI / Perplexity "best candy store near Grosse Pointe," "book a kids' candy party near Detroit," or "unique local corporate gift under $50" does not reliably name Sweet Pointe. That's the gap this closes — and it's measurable: we re-run the same prompts after launch.

We will not fabricate a visibility score — the baseline is established by running the real prompts at kickoff and recording who gets named.
