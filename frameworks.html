Every agent sits on a tier, and a tier only moves right when the undo is real, the ceiling exists, it has been boring for a while, and the log is legible:

The rules are written down in an Agent Constitution, and every agent in the portfolio runs under it. The gates are the trust. Speed comes from the agents; credibility comes from the checkpoints.

The Autonomy Gradient

Where the machine stops and you start.

The argument about AI autonomy usually gets framed as a dial: more or less, faster or safer. It isn't a dial. It's a line, and it sits between deciding and doing. Everything before it can be taken back. Everything after it spends money, time, or trust, and those don't come back.

Tier 0

Observe

Watches and logs. Changes nothing, claims nothing. You still do all the reading.

Where most teams are
Tier 1

Interpret

Tells you what changed and what it means, in your language, without five open tabs.

Signals · reconcile
Tier 2

Recommend

Ranks, routes, and drafts. Has an opinion about what matters most. Nothing has happened yet.

Prioritize · route · learn
Reversibility is the line
Tier 3

Act on approval

Does the thing once a person says yes. The yes is explicit, specific, and logged.

Approve · execute · record
Tier 4

Act alone

Acts without asking, inside limits set in advance. Only for actions that are cheap, reversible, and boring.

Earned, never assumed

Four permissions, not two

Every action an agent might take gets sorted before it ever runs. The width is roughly how much of an agent's day should live in each.

ALLOWruns alone
CONDITIONALinside a guardrail
APPROVALwaits for you
NEVERfixed list

Width ≈ share of an agent's day in each tier

Allow

Reversible, stays inside your own systems, touches no money.

Draft a report, tag files, refresh a tracker.

Conditional

Reversible, but crosses a line you set in advance. Runs alone while it stays inside the guardrail.

Publish from an approved calendar, nudge a bid within a set percentage.

Approval

Visible outside your team, or expensive to unwind. The agent prepares it; a person clicks send.

A client-facing report, a real budget increase.

Never

A short, fixed list. No context, urgency, or approval opens it up.

Moving money, unrecoverable deletes, changing account ownership.

Reversibility is the line.

A misread email costs nothing. A wrong priority costs a few minutes. A misrouted task costs a search.

A sent message cannot be unsent. A charge cannot be uncharged.

That's the whole rule. Not model confidence, not accuracy scores, not how impressive the demo was. Can it be taken back?

Left of the line · reversible

  • Reading, extracting, summarizing
  • Comparing sources and flagging conflicts
  • Ranking and proposing
  • Drafting anything, sending nothing
  • Filing into systems you control

Right of the line · spends something

  • Money, at any amount
  • Anything sent in your name
  • Anything that commits your time
  • Anything a third party will act on
  • Anything that touches a relationship
