How people search with AI. How they trust. How they decide.

By Amanda Friedt · November 27, 2026

The new boardroom conversation every CMO needs to lead.

TL;DR

  • Everything changed. How people search, how they trust, how they decide. Machines now shape that journey before people ever reach us.
  • When AI systems examine your brand, they don't read; they rewrite. If your data isn't clear and structured, the machine fills in the blanks with someone else's story.
  • No one owns the machine layer. Search owns a piece. Social owns a piece. PR owns a piece. Name a Machine Layer Owner.
  • Reframe marketing as infrastructure. Clean data. Structured content. Clear ownership. Each creative campaign brief should also include a machine layer brief.
  • Flexibility is the job now. Waiting for certainty is the fastest way to fall behind.

Written in late 2025; the argument holds, and the figures are dated where noted.

Why does every marketing cycle feel faster but somehow less effective? The pace is up, but the impact isn't keeping up, and every CMO in the room can feel that gap.

If you're leading marketing right now, you've probably had some meetings on repeat. Someone asks why things cost more, take longer, and feel harder even though the team is working twice as fast or hard. Probably with less budget and resources.

The short answer? Everything changed.

How people search. How they trust. How they decide.

Everything feels harder because the channels have multiplied, the algorithms have taken the front seat, and the visibility playbook has never caught up. Yet this moment also presents one of the biggest opportunities for brands in decades, if we're willing to evolve how we measure, structure, and present ourselves.

We can't continue to rely on outdated frameworks or operational playbooks. Marketing performance isn't about bar charts that move up and to the right anymore; it's about systems that adapt and connect. SEO has evolved into a dynamic, ongoing strategy. AI isn't just ChatGPT; it's transforming how people search, engage, and make decisions.

Messaging alone no longer builds trust or authority. What matters is how every part of your ecosystem interacts: how data, content, and channels work together in a constantly shifting environment. The traditional linear funnel no longer aligns with the way customers move. It's time to think holistically about the layered, omnichannel system we're actually operating in.

1. Start with business impact: how people search with AI

Skip the jargon. Forget "AI agents" and "reasoning systems." Say this instead:

"The way customers find and trust brands has changed. Machines now shape that journey before people ever reach us."

Whether it's reviews, Reddit threads, comparison searches, or output in AI tools. Machines influence today's customer journey. We must embrace the new realities in a way that impacts that journey for a positive outcome.

When AI systems examine your brand, they don't read; they rewrite. That means ensuring your story holds together everywhere machines examine the data, metadata, and content, all conveying the same message about what you stand for.

For CMOs, that's the new storytelling challenge: ensuring your brand's truth is consistent everywhere it lives, in the words, data, and systems that now shape visibility. The work is to keep that story human while making it machine-readable, so the algorithms don't flatten what makes you real.

If you've spent years building your brand authority, you can't afford to have machines flatten it. Because every time your story gets rewritten in an AI summary or search snippet, and it doesn't align with who you are, your customers, or their goals, a little of that hard-earned equity disappears. If your data isn't clear and structured, the machine fills in the blanks with someone else's story, and that rewrite decides your revenue, reputation, and reach.

Your customers have been rerouted

Consumers are no longer starting their searches on just Google or traditional search engines. In 2025, only 32.8% of global users reported discovering new brands through search engines (DataReportal, Digital 2025: how people discover new brands). That number used to be the heartbeat of brand discovery, and now it's a signal that people are finding brands through social feeds, chat-based AI, and recommendations long before they ever hit a search bar. When we examine Gen Z statistics, we see this even more clearly.

Among Gen Z, the mix flips:

Your customers are still searching and engaging; they've just been rerouted. If all of your eggs are still in one channel's basket, then we are missing the opportunity to start the conversation with a large portion of the consumer market. CMOs should be reallocating budgets and teams toward the spaces where discovery now begins: social algorithms, AI summaries, and machine-led recommendations, so the brand appears in the moment decisions are being made, not after.

2. Talk about brand visibility and trust: machines are now the gatekeepers

Machines now shape the journey before people ever reach the brand website.

The website and owned content channels don't just talk to Google anymore; they speak to every AI system interpreting your brand. If that data isn't clean or structured, those systems will fill in the blanks for you, and that means losing control of your own story.

Currently, brand visibility resides in the gaps

Search owns a piece. Social owns a piece. PR owns a piece. But no one owns the machine layer, the part where AI systems decide how your brand shows up, what it stands for, and whether it's even included in the conversation.

That's the new brand visibility gap: if you don't shape what AI thinks you are, they'll fill in the story themselves.

Owning visibility today means owning the data and structure that feed those systems. It's not a communications function or a media role; it's a cross-functional one.

  • Marketing defines the story.
  • Data teams shape the structure.
  • AI and engineering teams ensure the signals stay consistent across systems.

If nobody's accountable for that alignment, the machines will decide for you, and your competitors are owning your story, and they already are. You can see this if you research competitive terms using tools such as Perplexity or Claude. Examine comparison searches, price searches, and inquire about what each competitor and brand offers. The results will tell the story of what is already happening.

What does the machine owner look like?

The Machine Layer Owner is the person responsible for how your brand is perceived by algorithms, AI systems, search engines, and people. They ensure that your data, content, and signals convey a consistent story across the machine layer, maintaining visibility, trust, and relevance regardless of who or what is searching or looking.

Ask your team and leadership:

  • How do AI systems describe us right now?
  • Are we okay with what they're saying?
  • Who owns that visibility inside our company? How should we approach upskilling or adding new technology to support this? Define who owns the machine layer.

Why this matters. Trust has become a business lever, not a brand ideal.

The customer journey starts inside algorithms long before it reaches your website. Successful campaigns are gaining visibility because of the intersection of human and machine architecture. Earning trust with the modern consumer starts with a filter of choice.

We are already seeing a rise of "AI-handling roles," people who don't just use the technology but also shape how it works inside the business.

3. Reframe marketing as infrastructure

We build the foundation so the people behind the decisions can still find what's real about us, even in a world where machines do the introductions. Marketing today means building systems that keep your brand visible and steady, regardless of how rapidly the landscape shifts.

Clean data. Structured content. Clear ownership. Learning to teach AI is part of storytelling and creative art, because once we make the introduction, we still need to reach the human on the other end of the thread. Each creative campaign brief should also include a machine layer brief. We need to weave it into the thread of everything we do. It doesn't have to be complicated, but it does need to be in our systems.

Ask:

  • Are we investing in infrastructure or just activity?
  • Do we have the right people shaping how machines understand our brand?

4. Push for flexibility, not perfection

Chime's CMO told Business Insider in November 2025 that their team has cut campaign production time by 60% and reduced reliance on agencies by using AI to speed up creative processes (Business Insider). It wasn't about chasing efficiency for its own sake; it was about moving fast enough to stay seen as the algorithms shifted around them. Waiting for certainty is the fastest way to fall behind. Flexibility is the job now. We're all leading through shifting channels, data, and teams, with everything in motion at once.

The CMOs who'll embrace the opportunity and succeed are those who can move quickly, stay grounded, and keep their teams learning in real-time.

Ask:

  • How can we make space for testing and quick iteration?
  • What experiments are we running this quarter that future-proof us?

Brand visibility is established through orchestrating architecture and built through human and agentic connection. Help them understand the urgency without the fear. We don't need everyone to be an AI expert, but we do need to lead our brand forward.

Then lead them forward.

Remember, everyone's feeling this shift. Budgets aren't growing to match the pace of change. The ones who'll win now aren't the biggest; they're the ones who can move, learn, and adapt the fastest.

Brand checklist: leading the machine layer

Ownership

  • Assign a clear owner for machine-layer visibility, ideally a cross-functional lead who bridges brand, data, and AI.
  • Define their remit: maintain structured, accurate brand signals across every platform that feeds machines.

Implementation

  • Run an AI visibility audit: ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity how they describe your brand.
  • Align data, content, and AI teams to clean and structure key assets.
  • Start small: one flagship product or pillar page, then scale the approach.

Measurement

  • Track brand mentions and summaries in AI engines, not just search.
  • Monitor tone, accuracy, and inclusion in "top brand" responses.
  • Treat consistent, confident AI mentions as the new metric for visibility.

Upskilling

  • Build machine literacy training across marketing, data, and content.
  • Invest in schema, structured-data tools, and AI monitoring dashboards.
  • Teach teams to translate the story into signals machines can understand.

Competitive analysis

  • Compare how AI systems describe you versus competitors.
  • Document differences in tone, credibility, and ranking.
  • Use those insights to guide data and content updates.

Sources: DataReportal, Digital 2025; Marketing Dive, H1 2025; Business Insider, November 2025; Gartner 2025 CMO Spend Survey.
